# Risk module: Evidence and provenance

## Risk

Stale facts, citations that do not entail claims, abstract-only research summaries, and retrieval failure being mistaken for evidence of absence.

## Use when

Information may have changed, precise attribution matters, research evidence is requested, or source/version/population applicability affects the answer.

## Skip when

Stable background knowledge is sufficient and no current verification is requested.

## Invariants

- Identify the load-bearing claims and verify those claims, not merely the topic.
- Prefer primary or authoritative sources; check effective date separately from publication date.
- A citation must support the adjacent claim at the stated strength.
- For substantive academic synthesis, read the relevant full papers; disclose inaccessible key texts rather than inferring from abstracts.
- Distinguish direct result, author interpretation, synthesis, and speculation.
- Distinguish not searched, not found, inaccessible, and evidence of absence.

## Minimal checks

Retrieve the smallest sufficient source set, inspect the relevant body text, and compare population, intervention, outcome, version, jurisdiction, and date. Search for disconfirming evidence when it could change the decision.

## Escalate when

Sources conflict, the operative document is inaccessible, evidence is indirect, or high-stakes guidance depends on a weak link.
